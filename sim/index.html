Two predictions, simulated

The pipeline predicts how tightly a compound binds by folding the protein and the compound together and reading an affinity off the result. These are two of those predicted structures, put into explicit water at body temperature and allowed to move.

Both are the angiotensin II type 1 receptor, a membrane receptor of the family about a third of approved drugs act on. Both ran under the same protocol: 200 picoseconds with the protein held still so the water could find the pocket, the restraints released, then unrestrained dynamics. Five independent runs each, differing only in their starting velocities.

One prediction was almost exactly right. The other was wrong by nearly three thousand fold. Watch them before reading which is which.

the compound residues lining the pocket, every atom the rest of the receptor, as an alpha carbon trace

What the simulation was asked, and what it answered

The question was whether a prediction that falls apart under dynamics is a prediction whose affinity was wrong. If it were, a user with forty compounds could simulate them and know which five numbers to believe, which is the thing this platform cannot currently tell them.

It was registered in advance, including the test that had to pass before the full experiment ran: these two extremes had to separate in the predicted direction. They did not.

The prediction that was wrong by three thousand fold held its pose better than the one that was right, on both measures. And the gap between the two poses, 1.11 angstroms, is smaller than the 4.24 angstroms that five runs of the same pose scatter across, so the result is noise as well as backwards.

So the experiment stopped there. The registration required this pair to separate before 160 poses were simulated, and 16 GPU days were not spent. That is what writing the test down first is for.

Five candidate ways to tell a trustworthy prediction from an untrustworthy one have now been tested here and all five came back null: the model's own confidence in the structure, the disagreement between its two affinity heads, noise in the laboratory measurements being compared against, an arbitrary stereochemical choice affecting a fifth of predictions, and now stability under dynamics. The honest position is that the error is not concentrated anywhere we have found, and until it is, every prediction carries the same risk.

Each viewer plays 40 frames drawn from a 500 picosecond trajectory. The simulation ran with every atom, about a hundred thousand including water.
