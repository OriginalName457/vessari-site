What the corpus is made of

Protein family comes from UniProt molecular function keywords, which are curated and cited, so any one of them can be checked against the entry. It is not our reading of the protein name.

The composition is lopsided and the number matters more than the table. Fifty six of the 121 scored proteins are G protein coupled receptors. That is 46 percent of the corpus in one family, which happens because ChEMBL holds more measured compounds for receptors than for anything else and our selection rule needs at least 12 compounds that survive clustering.

A reviewer will ask whether the headline is really a statement about receptors. We checked. Median Spearman is 0.602 across the 56 receptors and 0.607 across the other 65, and median fold error is 4.6 and 4.1. The skew is real and it does not drive the result.

On disease, we have less than we would like. UniProt names a disease for 15 of the 121 and carries a disease comment for 37. An earlier version of this page grouped all 121 into therapeutic areas, which was our own reading of the names rather than anything a reader could check, so it is gone. Where UniProt names a disease it appears in the row. Where it does not, the row says nothing.

Why ··· did not score

A target is only scored if at least 12 of its measured compounds survive the selection rule, which keeps one compound per chemical cluster so that a held-out set cannot be eight members of the same series. Most proteins in ChEMBL do not have 12 compounds that far apart from each other. That requirement is why the corpus is this small.

This is worth stating plainly, because it is the limitation a reviewer should press on. The corpus is not a sample of proteins. It is the set of proteins with enough chemically diverse public measurements to score one, which is a different and much smaller population.
