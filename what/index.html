What this is, and what it is not

A laboratory with forty compound ideas and the budget to make five has to choose. Vessari predicts how tightly each one would bind its protein target, so the five are chosen on evidence rather than intuition. This page is for someone deciding whether that is worth funding, and it includes the parts that argue against.

How it works, in one paragraph

Give it a protein and a list of compounds. For each one it folds the protein and the compound together into a predicted three dimensional complex, then reads a binding affinity off that structure. It returns a ranked list with a calibrated interval on every number, and it refuses to answer where it cannot give one. The whole thing runs on three consumer graphics cards, not a cluster.

What is honestly established

A calibrated number where there was none

For a protein with a known sequence and no structure in the Protein Data Bank, the pipeline produces an affinity estimate and an interval that covers what it claims to cover. Nothing else in this project is as well supported as that.

It works on receptors, which was not obvious

Sixteen class A G protein coupled receptors were folded from sequence with no membrane and no experimental structure, and scored no worse than the soluble enzymes. That family is a large share of what approved drugs act on and the hardest to get structures for. We expected them to be the point where this stops working and published the prediction before measuring it.

Direction of change, against a far more expensive method

Across 4,899 within-target compound pairs, it calls the direction of an affinity change correctly 76.8 percent of the time. Free energy perturbation, which costs orders of magnitude more compute and needs hand parameterisation, scores 85.9 percent on the subset where both have an answer. We are worse, by nine points, for a tiny fraction of the cost and with no setup.

What is not established, stated plainly

That it beats a cheap two dimensional model

On the one target with a full control battery, a random forest trained on known actives alone scores 0.872 against our 0.284, and beats us 60.8 percent of the time trained on five compounds and 97.5 percent on twenty five. Every protein in our corpus has at least 190 known compounds, so every target we have measured sits where that cheaper model would win. The regime this method is supposed to be for, a protein with almost nothing known about it, we have never tested. An experiment to settle it is registered and running.

That we can say which predictions to trust

A user wants to know which five of forty numbers to believe. We cannot tell them. Five candidate signals have been tested and all five were null: the model's confidence in the structure it built, the disagreement between its two affinity heads, noise in the laboratory values, an arbitrary stereochemical choice affecting a fifth of predictions, and stability under molecular dynamics. Every prediction currently carries the same risk.

Anything prospective

Every result here is retrospective, on measurements that already existed. We tested whether accuracy holds on recently published compounds and could not run the primary test: ChEMBL's affinity data has a median publication year of 2004 and only 38 of 805 compounds were published after 2020. No prediction from this system has been tested at a bench. That is the gap a first collaboration would close.

Why the negative results are the asset

Anyone can report an accuracy figure. The question a reviewer actually has is whether to believe it. What is published here alongside the numbers:

Corrections log11 every number that moved after it was stated, and what moved it

Experiments halted by their own controls2 one saved 16 GPU days by failing a test written in advance
Published nulls5 candidate signals that did not work, reported rather than shelved
Retracted headline metrics2 including one found by auditing our own test and finding it circular

The sharpest example: the rule that no two compounds in a test set may belong to the same chemical series was documented one way and implemented another, so eight members of one series sat in a thirty compound set. We found it, measured what it cost, published it, and fixed the cause. A reviewer who finds a defect like that themselves discounts everything else. One who sees it found, measured and corrected has reason to trust the rest.

What funding would buy

The gating item is not compute and not code. It is one collaboration with a laboratory willing to test a handful of nominated compounds at the bench. That converts every claim here from retrospective to prospective, and it is the single change that would most alter how this work should be judged.
