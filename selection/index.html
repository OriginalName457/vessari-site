Which five to make

A laboratory looking at thirty compounds can afford to synthesise five. That is the decision this method exists to inform, so this page measures it directly rather than through an error statistic.

For every protein below, the held-out compounds were ranked twice. Once by the affinity measured in a laboratory, which is the answer. Once by the affinity this method predicted from a folded structure, which is the guess. The question is how many of the genuinely best five appear in the five we would have nominated.

What the number covers

These held-out sets are built to spread evenly across the affinity range, so the best five are separable by construction. A real campaign often tests variations on one idea, where the compounds sit much closer together in potency and the ranking is harder. This figure is not a promise about an arbitrary list.

The sets were also built under a clustering rule later found to be weaker than the one documented, which let members of one chemical series into a set. The corpus is being re-measured under the corrected rule and this page should be recomputed when that finishes. What is already measured on 27 of those targets is a mean drop in rank correlation of 0.038 with an interval of [-0.086, +0.004], so expect this number to move a little and downward.

And every result here is retrospective. No compound was nominated by this method and then made.

The per-protein data on this page is targets.json, 119 proteins and 2,761 compounds as the runs wrote them. The summary figures and the caveats are in top5-selection.json.
