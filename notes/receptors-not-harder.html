The receptors were not harder

The corpus went from twelve scored proteins to twenty eight. Sixteen of the new ones are class A G protein coupled receptors, which we expected to be the point where this stops working. They scored no worse than the soluble enzymes.

Everything this project claims rests on a corpus of proteins the pipeline has been scored against, one entry each, with the held-out set built so no two compounds share an ECFP4 Tanimoto cluster at 0.6. That rule exists because splitting by Bemis-Murcko scaffold let eight members of one congeneric series into a thirty compound set and moved a Spearman from 0.248 to 0.564. The corpus was twelve. It is now twenty eight, with eleven more proteins the worker declined and recorded a reason for.

What the twenty eight scored

Mean Spearman 0.55, bootstrap interval over targets [0.467, 0.625]. At twelve targets the same figure was 0.538 with an interval of [0.430, 0.636]. More than doubling the corpus moved the mean by 0.012 and narrowed the interval, which is the result we wanted and not the one a cherry picked twelve would have given. 18 of 28 are above 0.5 and 1 is below zero.

The receptors

A G protein coupled receptor has seven transmembrane helices and sits in a lipid bilayer. The pipeline folds it from sequence with no membrane, no lipid, and no experimental structure, then reads an affinity off the pose. There is a straightforward reason to expect that to fail, and the day before this run I wrote that it probably would, that the receptors would pull the mean down, and that this was the point of measuring them.

Class A GPCRs16 +0.585 +0.568 4.2x
Everything else12 +0.503 +0.496 4.6x

The receptors came out +0.082 ahead, with an interval on that difference of [-0.079, +0.249] and a rank sum test at p = 0.318. The interval straddles zero, so the honest reading is not that receptors are easier. It is that they are not harder, and that was the claim in doubt. The two best scoring receptors are the P2Y purinoceptor 12 at 0.813 and the beta-2 adrenergic receptor at 0.798, both above every soluble enzyme in the corpus except two.

This matters more than one more row in a table. Receptors of this family are a large share of the proteins that approved drugs act on, and they are the ones least likely to have a crystal structure to work from. A method that needs an experimental structure cannot be pointed at most of them. This one was pointed at sixteen and did not degrade.

Two cautions belong next to that. Sixteen receptors is enough to rule out a large penalty and not enough to rule out a small one, and all sixteen are class A, which is the best characterised of the GPCR classes and the one with the most structures in the training data for any model of this kind. Neither of those is addressed by measuring more class A receptors.

The worst result in the corpus

Two paralogues of the same human enzyme sit at opposite ends. 3-oxo-5-alpha-steroid 4-dehydrogenase 1 scored +0.513 and its paralogue 2 scored -0.100 with a median error of 28.8 fold, the only negative entry here. They are the same fold acting on the same substrate. Whatever separates them is not protein family, size, or compound count, and we do not currently know what it is. It is recorded rather than explained.
