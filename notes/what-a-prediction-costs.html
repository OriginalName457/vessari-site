What a prediction costs

Sixty two seconds and four watt hours of card energy per compound, measured on the rig rather than divided out of a total. Three cards do not make it three times faster, and this note says why.

The folding figure is a batch of six compounds in 370 seconds with the alignment already cached. Across the 2,462 complexes in the published corpus the median is 78 seconds, with the tenth percentile at 66 and the ninetieth at 120, so 62 is the fast end of a real spread and the spread comes from protein length.

The relaxation figure is the median of 190 poses, from 6 seconds to 36. It is only run on a pose that fails a check. One hundred and ninety six of our 658 complexes failed one, so the tables below cost relaxation at 30 percent of compounds.

The alignment is paid once per protein and cached on disk. It was being fetched once per compound until 16 October, which put 150 requests on the link for one protein in a 150 compound screen. Removing that did not change the wall clock, because the alignment call runs while the card works.

The published figure is 20 seconds

The Boltz-2 preprint states approximately 20 seconds to score one ligand. We measure 62 on an RTX 3090. The paper does not say whether its figure includes the alignment and the structure pass, and the sentence appears in a section about generative screening where a cached receptor is plausible. We are three times slower than the number a reader will take from the paper, and every throughput figure on this site uses ours.

Three cards, one lane

The rig has three RTX 3090s and 15.9 GB of system memory. One folding job reserves 11.2 GB of that, so the pipeline holds an exclusive lock and runs one job at a time. Two together is an out of memory kill, which cost four predictions on 14 October and is why the lock exists. The constraint here is the host, so none of the times below divide by three.

Two of the four memory slots on this board are empty. Filling them is the difference between one lane and three, and until they are filled the ceiling below is the ceiling.

Sixty compounds is the size of the laboratory validation set in our protocol, and it takes an hour and a quarter. A thousand compounds is under a day. Ten thousand is eight days and that is the honest edge of what this hardware screens, which is why the screen we registered puts a fingerprint model in front of the folding stage and sends it only the top 150.

Ten thousand compounds draws 41.7 kWh, which costs a few dollars at any domestic rate. The figure a buyer should price is the eight days of wall clock. Filling the two empty memory slots is what moves it.

Every number here is in compute-cost.json, with how each one was measured. Card power is a mean of 242 watts sampled under folding load against a 390 watt limit. The host draw is not metered, so the watt hours above are card only and the true figure is higher.
