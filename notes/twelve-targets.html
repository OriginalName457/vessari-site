Twelve targets instead of two

Every performance number on this site came from two proteins. An unattended run took that to twelve. This is what the twelve scored, and what the run cost us on the way.

The corpus worker builds a series-disjoint held-out set for one target, predicts every compound in it, scores the result and appends the scores to ~/.vessari/corpus/corpus.jsonl before taking the next target. It ran unattended on 29 September 2026, finishing its first target at 18:47 UTC and its last at 00:07 UTC the following day, 5.81 hours of scored work summed across targets on one rig. Sixteen entries came out of it: twelve targets scored, four declined with a stated reason.

Scoped to twelve proteins that passed a length and compound-count filter. Not a claim about proteins outside it.

Each held-out set keeps one compound per ECFP4 Tanimoto cluster at 0.6, single linkage, so no two compounds in a set belong to the same series. The largest pairwise similarity anywhere in the twelve sets is 0.597. Across the twelve, 241 compounds were scored out of 306 predicted; the other 65 returned no affinity value at all. Of the 241, 146 landed within ten fold of the measured affinity.

Mean Spearman over the twelve targets is 0.538, 95% CI [0.430, 0.636], bootstrapped by resampling targets rather than compounds. The median of the twelve per-target median fold errors is 5.0x, 95% CI [3.9x, 11.2x] on the same bootstrap.

Four of the twelve are G protein coupled receptors, which this pipeline had never been measured on. Beta-2 adrenergic receptor, at 0.798 on 25 compounds, is the second best target in the corpus. The other three came in at 0.534 on 22 compounds, 0.490 on 24 and 0.091 on 10, which is most of the range the whole corpus covers. As far as these numbers go the receptors do not behave as a group.

Four further targets were declined rather than scored, because too few compounds survived clustering to build a set: peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase B left 1 compound, beta-lactamase TEM 5, UDP-N-acetylglucosamine 1-carboxyvinyltransferase 5 and adenosine deaminase 6, against a floor of 12. A declined target is written to the corpus with its reason rather than dropped, because the reason measures where the selection rule bites.

What the interval covers, and what it does not.

The interval is now around a claim about the method

The held-out figures published here before this run were Spearman 0.284 on 30 ABL1 compounds and 0.537 on 24 acetylcholinesterase compounds. Each came with an interval, and each of those intervals was an interval on one protein. It said how uncertain we were about ABL1, not how uncertain we were about the next protein a reader might bring. With two targets there was no way to put a number on the second question at all.

Twelve is enough to resample targets, so [0.430, 0.636] is the first figure on this site that describes the pipeline rather than a protein. It is also the one that matters to anyone deciding whether to point this at their own target, and it is wide. The number to plan against is the lower end, 0.430.

The twelve were not drawn from proteins in general. They passed the queue filter in src/vessari/corpus/queue.py: 150 to 420 residues, between 40 and 4,000 measured compounds in ChEMBL, and enough of those surviving clustering to fill a set of at least 12. An interval over twelve targets selected that way says nothing about a protein the filter would have rejected.

Our own failure. It is inside every average above.

Histamine H2 receptor came back at 0.091

On 10 held-out compounds the rank correlation is 0.091, 95% CI [−0.639, 0.795]. That is a failure. Calling it noise because the interval is wide would be the wrong reading: ten compounds cannot show much, and ten compounds is what this target had after clustering, so the width is part of the result rather than an excuse for it. The median fold error on those ten is 5.1x, near the corpus median of 5.0x, so the predicted affinities are not far off in magnitude. They are in the wrong order.

H2 is a 359 residue receptor, inside the length range, with enough measured compounds in ChEMBL to enter the queue. Nothing marked it in advance. It is the case to hold against the mean: one target in twelve returned a ranking with no usable signal, and we could not have said which one beforehand.

Our own error. Eleven entries deleted from the corpus before any number above was computed.

A database outage was recorded as a property of six proteins

The worker's first attempt ran while ChEMBL was returning HTTP 500. The set builder treated any non-200 response as the end of the result list, found zero compounds, and the worker wrote "scoring failed" against the target and marked it permanently skipped. Eleven such entries were written in two bursts, the first five of them inside 48 seconds by their own timestamps. Five of the six targets involved scored normally once ChEMBL recovered and are in the table above. The sixth, peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase B, was later declined for a reason that holds.

An upstream failure and an unusable target need opposite handling. A target whose chemistry this pipeline cannot process should be skipped and never retried. A target whose database we could not reach should be left exactly where it was. The worker had one path for both. There is now an Upstream exception that returns the target to the queue and waits, and a check against the activity count recorded when the target was enqueued, so finding zero compounds where the queue says there were hundreds counts as a contradiction rather than an answer. The fix is in scripts/corpus_worker.py. All eleven entries were deleted before the analysis on this page ran.

A trap we walked into. The rule was ours and it was in our own materials.

The length rule came from two points and does not survive twelve

Target selection has bounded protein length at 150 to 420 residues since the queue was written. That bound came from two measurements: ABL1's kinase domain at 254 residues gave interface pTM above 0.80 throughout, and acetylcholinesterase at 583 residues gave a median of 0.27 with every compound below the worst ABL1 pose. One good point and one bad point were turned into a rule about length, and the rule was then repeated in our own materials as guidance that proteins under about 400 residues are comfortable for this pipeline.

Measured across the twelve, the correlation between protein length and per-target Spearman is −0.336, over lengths of 159 to 419 residues. That is weak, and it rests on twelve targets, so it does not rule a length effect out. What it does is remove the basis for the sentence we were writing. Inside 159 to 419 residues, length does not predict whether this pipeline will rank a target's compounds: the best target in the corpus is 271 residues and the second best is 413.

The bound itself stays, because nothing here tests it. Every target in the corpus was inside 150 to 420 residues by construction, so the corpus says nothing about the span from 420 to 583 that the bound was invented to cover.

Reproduce: the scores are one JSON object per target in ~/.vessari/corpus/corpus.jsonl, written by scripts/corpus_worker.py. The aggregate figures resample the twelve targets with replacement over 20,000 draws. The deleted entries and the fix behind them are logged in CORRECTIONS.md.

Twelve targets is not fifty. The worker takes another target whenever the rig is idle, so the numbers on this page will move. When they move, both versions go in the corrections log.
