Our error in kilocalories

Our headline is a 4.6 fold median absolute error, which is 0.904 kilocalories per mole. Free energy perturbation is quoted in those units, so somebody will lay the two side by side. This note says which comparison holds and which does not, and gives the one number that is measured the same way on the same pairs.

The conversion

A fold error in a dissociation constant becomes an energy through RT ln of the fold. At 298.15 K, RT is 0.59248 kilocalories per mole. So 4.6 fold is 0.904 kcal/mol, and our published interval of 4.3 to 5.0 fold is 0.864 to 0.954 kcal/mol.

What that number cannot be compared with

A hand tuned FEP+ protocol reaches a weighted pairwise RMSE of 1.20 kcal/mol on a large public benchmark, as recomputed by the Open Free Energy consortium. Printing 0.904 next to 1.20 would say we beat it. We do not, and three differences make that comparison empty.

The first is single point against pairwise. Ours is the error in one predicted affinity against one measured affinity. Theirs is the error in the difference between two compounds, which is the quantity a medicinal chemist uses and the harder one to get right, because the errors in the two endpoints do not cancel.

The second is median absolute error against root mean square error. A median ignores the tail. An RMSE is dominated by it. Our own distribution has a long tail, so the two statistics are not the same claim about the same predictions.

The third is the measurement underneath. We fetch IC50 and Ki values in nanomolar with an exact relation from ChEMBL and reduce the repeats for one compound to a geometric median. We do not fetch Kd. We also do not record which type each value was, so our headline pools inhibition constants and binding constants without distinguishing them. An IC50 is not a binding constant: it depends on the substrate or radioligand concentration in that assay through the Cheng-Prusoff relation, which we have not applied. The free energy benchmarks are curated to one assay type per series. Recording the type per activity is a small change to the fetcher and it is on the list.

The comparison that does hold

We already measure the pairwise quantity. Every held-out set is turned into within-target compound pairs, and the error in the predicted difference is scored in kilocalories per mole, which is the same statistic FEP+ reports.

On 518 pairs where both we and FEP+ have an answer, our pairwise RMSE is 1.31 kcal/mol against 1.012 for FEP+. We get the direction of the change right 76.8 percent of the time against 85.9 percent. Same pairs, same statistic, and we are 0.30 kcal/mol behind a method that needs hand parameterisation and orders of magnitude more compute.

The RMSE rises as the threshold rises because a larger real change is a larger number to get wrong. The column beside it is what you would score by predicting that nothing changes, which is the floor any method has to clear. We clear it at every threshold.

The sentence to use

Our single point error is 0.904 kcal/mol and it is not comparable to a free energy perturbation number. Our pairwise error is 1.31 kcal/mol on the pairs FEP+ also scored, where FEP+ reaches 1.012. Quote the second one when the comparison is to a physics method.

Every figure here reads from paired-validation.json and corpus-123-targets.json, which are what the runs wrote. The FEP+ figure of 1.20 kcal/mol comes from 10.1021/acs.jcim.6c00089, which recomputes previously published FEP+ runs on the Open Free Energy system list rather than running FEP+ fresh, and the 1.012 figure is FEP+ on our own head to head subset.
