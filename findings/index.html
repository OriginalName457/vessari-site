Critical findings

A log of things that did not work. Some are claims we tested and could not reproduce. Some are traps in tools everyone uses. Several are our own numbers, which moved after we had already published them.

Each entry says in its first line what kind of finding it is. Where somebody established a result before us, they are named there and not in a footnote. A replication is still worth writing down, because a result that only exists in one paper is a result nobody has checked, but it is not a discovery and will not be dressed as one.

Our own error. Three targets said yes, fifteen said no.

The model's confidence in a pose does not predict how wrong the affinity is

When the model places a ligand it reports its own confidence in that interface. We built a trust signal on it: poses above 0.90 were labelled good, below 0.60 poor, and the affinities sorted accordingly. The evidence was three targets, where the correlation between interface confidence and error averaged about -0.57. Lower confidence, worse number, which is what it should do.

At fifteen targets and 325 predictions the correlation averages -0.067, with a bootstrap interval over targets of -0.209 to +0.085. Nine of fifteen are negative, which is what a coin does. The first three targets were not a sample, they were the three we happened to run first, and they were the favourable tail of a distribution sitting on zero. Re-measured now, one of those three has changed sign.

The worst-case version of the claim fails separately. We said a pose above 0.90 is never wrong by more than about 400 fold. Eight of 244 such poses exceed it, the worst by 132,867 fold at a confidence of 0.961. A bound with counterexamples is not a bound.

One piece looked like it survived and does not. Predictions below 0.60 really do have a higher median error, 43 fold against 5, and the test on that returns p = 0.0005. All 23 of them come from one protein. The comparison is acetylcholinesterase against fourteen others, so it measures that acetylcholinesterase is hard, which its affinities already told us.

The thresholds are removed rather than retuned. Fitting new ones to the 325 predictions that refuted the old ones would produce numbers that describe this corpus and promise nothing about the next target. Interface confidence is still recorded on every prediction and still shown to a reader, because it is the model's own statement about the structure it built. Nothing downstream weights, gates or filters on it, and predicting which affinities are wrong is back to being an open problem here.

Reproduce: per-target correlations over ~/.vessari/corpus/*/predictions.json plus the three named held-out sets in data. The retraction and every number in it are logged in CORRECTIONS.md.

Our own error. Published for a day before we caught it.

A sentence we put on this site was false, and the effect it described was real

We wrote that eight congeneric compounds admitted by a Bemis-Murcko split had taken every sub-nanomolar slot in a thirty compound held-out set. Five compounds in that set are sub-nanomolar. Three of them belong to the series. The series spans 0.34 to 31.95 nM, a 94 fold range, so it is not concentrated at the potent end at all.

The sentence was written as a plausible mechanism for a real effect and never checked against the compound list. Two neighbouring details were also loose: seven of the eight share a benzamide rather than eight, and six of the eight differ only in the fused heteroaryl cap.

What survives is the effect, and the test that establishes it is a matched null rather than the story. Keeping one compound per ECFP4 Tanimoto cluster gives Spearman 0.250. Dropping the same nine compounds at random, ignoring clusters, leaves it at 0.556. A random subset does as badly as declustering 0.25 percent of the time, so the collapse is specific to removing the series.

Reproduce: scripts/score_holdout.py against abl1-holdout-predictions.json. Every number that has moved is logged in CORRECTIONS.md.

Replication of Steshin 2023 and Guo, Hernández-Hernández and Ballester 2024. Not a new result.

Murcko scaffold splitting let a ponatinib analogue series into our held-out set

Splitting a held-out set by Bemis-Murcko scaffold is the default across this field. It does not separate close analogues, and that was established before we ran into it. Steshin showed in the Lo-Hi benchmark that after a scaffold split, most test molecules still have a close training neighbour, including 78 percent of one TDC CYP set. Guo and colleagues put the result in a title across 2,100 models on 60 datasets.

Our case adds a mechanism and a magnitude on a co-folding model. Eight compounds in our ABL1 set share a benzamide, a piperazinylmethyl, a trifluoromethyl and an internal diaryl alkyne, and differ in a fused heteroaryl cap. That cap sits inside the ring system, so Murcko writes eight different scaffold strings for what a chemist reads as one ponatinib-like series, and the one-compound-per-scaffold rule admitted all eight. Reported Spearman fell from 0.564 to 0.250 when the set was collapsed to one compound per cluster.

We log it because it broke one of our own published numbers, not because it is news. If you build held-out sets on Murcko scaffolds, the number you are reporting is probably higher than the one you meant to report.

Compounds: CHEMBL2316579, 2316581, 2316582, 2316585, 2316870, 2316871, 2324924, 2324925. Selection rule now in src/vessari/holdout/build.py.

A trap we walked into. The behaviour is documented; the consequence is not obvious.

Training one model across several TDC ADMET endpoints voids their scaffold splits

The TDC ADMET Benchmark Group scores 22 endpoints on scaffold splits, so a leaderboard entry is meant to report how a model handles chemotypes it has not seen. Each endpoint is split on its own. Many of them share molecules, and the CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 Veith sets come from a single screening campaign, so they share most of a compound library by construction.

We measured the overlap before building a shared-trunk model and did not build it. Of cyp2d6_veith's 2,626 test molecules, 1,759 appear in cyp3a4_veith's training set. That is 67 percent. Across the suite, 462 endpoint pairs share at least one molecule across that boundary. A model trained on all endpoints at once and scored on their individual test splits is fitted on labelled copies of most of its own test set.

Multitask ADMET models are common and this check is not part of the usual recipe. Ours is single task for this reason, which costs us the transfer that makes multitask attractive on the sparse endpoints.

Reproduce with the official TDC archive through src/vessari/admet/dataset.py. The overlap is a property of the published splits, not of anything we did to them.

Replication of documented behaviour, with one measurement of our own.

In Boltz-2, diffusion_samples_affinity is a best-of-N pose search rather than an averaging ensemble

The flag reads like an ensemble size. It is not one. The affinity stage draws N poses, ranks them by interface pTM, and conditions the affinity head on the single best. The other N minus one never reach it and are not averaged into the reported value.

This matters if you raise the flag expecting variance to fall as one over the square root of N. It will not. What did remove our run-to-run variance was a seed, which Boltz does not set by default: three runs of one compound with a fixed seed returned identical values to sixteen significant figures, while unseeded runs of the same input differed by about 0.05 log units. Averaging, if you want it, has to happen outside the tool across independent runs.

The selection is at boltz/model/models/boltz2.py, an argsort on ipTM taking index zero. Our replicate averaging is in adapters.predict_replicates.

Scoped to our use. Not a claim that the tool is wrong.

Why we do not gate proposed analogues on synthetic accessibility score

Across the 19 imatinib analogues our generator produces, RDKit's SA_Score spans 2.350 to 2.533 against the parent's 2.332. That is 0.18 units on a scale of 1 to 10. We are not claiming an error: one-atom analogues of a marketed drug plausibly are of near-identical difficulty, and the score may be right about that.

We are claiming it cannot answer the question we have, which is how much of the parent's synthetic route survives the change. Two observations shaped that. The score rates hexa-tert-butylbenzene, a molecule that has never been made and is sterically impossible, at 2.16, below imatinib's 2.33. And it rated a disconnected molecule produced by a bug in our own generator, a piperazine sitting beside a separate fragment, at 2.46. Used as a gate it would have passed that bug, which three other checks also passed.

What we use instead is a route class stated per transformation: whether the change can plausibly be made on the finished molecule, whether it arrives on a different purchased building block, or whether it breaks the sequence. That is chemistry written down rather than computed, and it is narrower and more useful than a score.

RDKit 2026.03.5, rdkit.Contrib.SA_Score. Route classes in src/vessari/design/synthesis.py.

Our own error, caught before it reached anyone.

A gene symbol is not a protein, and our lookup assumed it was human

An assessment of InhA, the Mycobacterium tuberculosis enoyl reductase that isoniazid targets, came back describing human inhibin alpha. Both carry the symbol InhA and they are unrelated: a 269 residue bacterial enzyme and a 366 residue reproductive hormone subunit. Our protein lookup restricted every search to human, so it answered confidently and wrongly, and the dossier carried one protein's gene record and literature beside binding numbers computed from the other.

Two attempted fixes were worse than the bug. Widening the search to every organism answers ABL1 with a mouse orthologue as readily as the human protein. Refusing whenever candidates differ in length blocked ABL1 outright, because a fungus has a gene of that name too, and a tool that refuses on every homonym teaches people to skip its warnings.

What works is to proceed and name the species. InhA now resolves to the human protein and says so in the output, which makes the error impossible to miss, and an accession pins the one you meant. Eight organisms carry that symbol.

Ranking and the cross-organism listing are in src/vessari/data/lookup.py.
